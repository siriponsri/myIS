# Academic Writing Guardrails

When writing, revising, or reviewing academic text, follow these rules:

1. **Avoid formulaic academic phrases.**
   Do not repeatedly use templates such as “This study aims to…”, “It is important to note…”, “The findings highlight…”, or “This study contributes…”.

2. **Limit contrast templates.**
   Avoid overusing “not X, but Y”, “not only X but also Y”, “rather than X”, and similar constructions. Use them only when the contrast matters to the argument.

3. **Do not overuse em dashes.**
   Avoid recurring patterns such as `X—rather than Y—shows Z`. Prefer normal sentence structures when possible.

4. **Avoid automatic three-part lists.**
   Do not default to rhetorical triples such as “accuracy, robustness, and efficiency”. The number of items should follow the actual argument.

5. **Do not mechanically start paragraphs with transitions.**
   Avoid repeated openings such as “Moreover”, “Furthermore”, “Additionally”, “However”, “Therefore”, and “Taken together”.

6. **Prefer concrete propositions over academic-sounding filler.**
   Every important claim should say something that can be tested, challenged, or supported with evidence. Remove vague statements with low informational value.

7. **Do not restate the same idea using synonyms.**
   Repetition is justified only when adding new evidence, mechanism, comparison, boundary condition, result, or implication.

8. **Maintain an evidence-based position.**
   Do not artificially balance every argument with equal pros and cons. State which interpretation is better supported and explain why.

9. **Synthesize literature instead of listing papers.**
   Organize related work around disagreements, assumptions, mechanisms, limitations, or unresolved problems, not author-by-author summaries.

10. **Make contributions specific.**
    Avoid generic claims such as “fills an important gap”. Explain exactly what changes: an explanation, mechanism, boundary condition, evaluation method, system design, or interpretation of prior evidence.

11. **Never invent citations.**
    Verify authors, title, year, venue, DOI/URL, and that the cited work actually supports the claim. If verification is unavailable, flag the citation for checking.

12. **Let argument shape the prose.**
    Do not force every paragraph into `topic sentence → explanation → example → conclusion`. Allow anomalies, counter-evidence, assumptions, failure cases, and changes in analytical level when they matter.

## Core Rule

**Prioritize clear propositions, evidence, mechanisms, and defensible reasoning over polished academic-sounding prose.**

During revision, actively detect repeated rhetorical patterns across the entire paper, not only within individual paragraphs.
